**The idle auto-off records each worker's last capture count, so a worker that boots, works and powers off between two hourly polls is counted (#3208, found on #3205).** The capture ledger (`runs/fleet-captures-state.json`) was fed only by `fleet-watch.mjs`'s hourly poll, and a worker is up for its job plus the 5-minute idle threshold, so a short job's worker came and went between two polls and its captures vanished with its process (arithmetic, not measured: a 10-minute job is seen with probability 25%, a 1-minute job 10%). `fleet-auto-off.mjs` already probes every up worker's `/health` every 10 s until it powers it off; `probeIdle` now also carries `vitals.captures` and `vitals.uptimeMinutes` (only when numeric), and each tick feeds them to the same `advanceCaptures` through `fleet-watch.mjs`'s now-exported `recordCaptures`, rows named `<name>  <address>` as `fleet-status` names them so both writers share a key. A worker that did not answer, or answered without a count, records nothing. Both writers go through `recordCaptures`, which reads, advances and writes the ledger under an exclusive lock file (`<ledger>.lock`, broken after 2 s as a dead writer's) and replaces the file by staging file and rename, so neither a half file nor the other writer's interleaved snapshot can drop a reading. A ledger that cannot be written is reported on stderr and never stops a shutdown decision. No threshold, window or idle decision moves.
